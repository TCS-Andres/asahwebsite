## The Question That Comes Up at the Orthodontic Consult

Fall is a busy season for orthodontic consults. Your ten-year-old smiles for the intraoral photos, and someone mentions the small band of tissue between the upper lip and the gum. Or maybe you're the one in the chair, an adult finally looking into braces, and you catch yourself wondering whether a lip tie in adults is a real thing.

It is, and it's also very common to have a prominent upper lip frenum that never causes a single problem. Many people have one and never need anything done. What matters is not how the tissue looks, but whether it plays a part in a real concern with the teeth, the gums, cleaning, or orthodontic plans.

## What Does a Lip Tie Look Like in an Older Child or Adult?

Gently lift your upper lip in a mirror and you'll see a thin fold of tissue running from the inside of the lip to the gum above the front teeth. That's the maxillary labial frenum. Everyone has one, and its size and attachment point vary widely.

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry describes several attachment levels, from high on the gum to fibers that extend down between the two front teeth. A lower, thicker attachment may be the kind people call a lip tie. Even so, appearance alone doesn't tell you much.

### Signs That May Be Worth Mentioning

Some people notice a lip that feels tight or doesn't lift easily, gum tissue that blanches (turns pale) when the lip is pulled up, plaque or food that tends to collect above the front teeth, or a gap between the upper front teeth that persists. None of these confirms a lip tie, but they are worth a look.

## Can a Lip Tie Cause a Gap Between Front Teeth?

Sometimes it can be one contributor, but it's rarely the whole story. A gap between the upper front teeth, called a midline diastema, is very common in young children. Dental literature notes that many of these gaps get smaller on their own as the permanent lateral incisors and canines come in.

A low, fibrous frenum is one of several possible reasons a gap stays open. Tooth size, jaw growth, missing teeth, and habits such as tongue thrust can all play a part too. A gap by itself is not a reason for a release. Our guide to [when a child should first see an orthodontist](/first-orthodontic-visit-age-7/) explains why early checkups focus on watching growth.

For adults, a release alone isn't expected to close an existing gap. When it is part of the plan, it's usually considered alongside orthodontic treatment, with the orthodontist moving the teeth.

## Can a Lip Tie Cause Gum Recession or Trouble Brushing?

It may play a role for some people. A frenum that attaches close to the gumline can pull on the gum tissue when the lip moves, and clinicians consider that one possible contributor to recession above the front teeth.

Recession has many common causes, though. Cleveland Clinic lists brushing too hard, plaque and tartar buildup, gum disease, misaligned teeth, tobacco use, lip piercings, and naturally thin gum tissue. A careful exam considers all of these first.

When the lip is hard to lift, the area where it attaches can also be tricky to brush well. The AAPD has noted that the link between a prominent labial frenum and cavities or gum disease still needs more research, so this is a reason to look, not a reason to worry.

## Before or After Braces? Why Timing Is a Team Decision

This is one of the most common questions parents ask, and clinicians don't all answer it the same way. A 2025 narrative review in Dentistry Journal found little evidence to support early lip frenum surgery in children. Its authors suggested that waiting until the permanent upper canines erupt, typically between about 9 and 12 years of age, may help avoid regrowth and complications.

Other clinicians favor earlier release in select cases, and published case reports describe both approaches. Because the evidence is still developing, the decision works best as a conversation between your family, your orthodontist, and the clinician evaluating the tie.

Retainers and resting tongue posture also matter for stability, as our post on [teeth shifting after braces](/teeth-shifting-after-braces/) explains. A release may help support a stable result for some patients, but no one can promise it.

## Do Adults Need Lip Tie Surgery? When a Lip Tie in Adults Is Worth a Look

Most adults with a prominent frenum don't need surgery. A release is typically considered only when the tissue seems to contribute to a real concern, not for appearance or prevention alone.

An evaluation makes sense if a gap between your front teeth has persisted and you're planning orthodontic treatment, if your dentist or hygienist has noted recession or pulling at the gum above your front teeth, or if you consistently have trouble keeping that area clean. A lip that feels tight when you smile or eat is worth mentioning too.

If your child's orthodontist raises the frenum, or a gap remains after the canines come in, that's a good time to ask. Many kids with a lip tie also have their tongue checked, and our post on [tongue tie in older children](/tongue-tie-older-kids/) covers that side of the exam. As Dr. Culotta puts it, "Positive change is possible at any age. Awareness is the first step."

## What You Can Track Before a Visit

A few notes and photos make a consultation far more useful.

* **Photos:** a clear, well-lit photo of the front teeth with the lip relaxed, and one with the lip gently lifted.
* **Gap history:** when you first noticed any space between the front teeth, and whether it has changed.
* **Gums and cleaning:** any recession, bleeding, or plaque buildup your dentist or hygienist has mentioned above the front teeth.
* **Orthodontic plans:** your orthodontist's notes, the planned start date, and any comments about the frenum.
* **Lip comfort:** whether the lip feels tight when smiling, eating, or speaking, and whether it bothers you or your child.

If a release does become part of the plan, therapy is often part of the picture too. Our guide to [myofunctional therapy before and after a tie release](/myofunctional-therapy-tongue-tie-release/) explains how that work fits in.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a lip tie cause a gap between front teeth?

A lip tie can be one of several contributors to a gap between the upper front teeth, but many gaps in children narrow on their own as permanent teeth come in. An exam looks at the whole picture.

### Can a lip tie cause gum recession?

A frenum attached close to the gumline may contribute to recession above the front teeth for some people, but recession has many other causes. Brushing too hard, gum disease, and thin tissue are common factors.

### Should my child's lip tie be released before or after braces?

Timing depends on your child's growth and orthodontic plan, and many clinicians prefer to wait until the permanent canines erupt. A 2025 review supported that conservative approach, so this is best decided together with your orthodontist.

### Does a lip tie in adults need to be treated?

Most adult lip ties never need treatment, and a release is usually considered only when the tissue contributes to a gum, cleaning, comfort, or orthodontic concern. An in-person exam helps sort out whether it plays a role.

### What is recovery like after a lip tie release?

Recovery after a lip tie release varies from person to person, and aftercare stretches and follow-up visits are an important part of healing well. Our post on [adult tongue tie release recovery](/adult-tongue-tie-release-recovery/) covers what the healing process can involve.
