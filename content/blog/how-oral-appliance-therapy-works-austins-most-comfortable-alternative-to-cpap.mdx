## What Is Oral Appliance Therapy?

Oral appliance therapy uses a small, custom-made device worn in the mouth during sleep. Its primary function is to keep the airway open by repositioning the lower jaw or tongue so that breathing remains unobstructed throughout the night.

These devices resemble a retainer or mouthguard but are custom-fitted to the patient's unique anatomy. They are FDA-approved for treating obstructive sleep apnea and have been proven effective in reducing snoring, improving oxygen flow, and increasing sleep quality.

What an oral appliance does:

- Keeps the lower jaw slightly forward to prevent airway collapse
- Stabilizes the tongue to reduce obstruction
- Improves airflow through the upper airway
- Reduces or eliminates snoring
- Minimizes apnea and hypopnea episodes

For many Austin patients, it provides the freedom and comfort CPAP cannot.

## Why Oral Appliance Therapy Works

Obstructive sleep apnea occurs when the airway becomes blocked during sleep. The blockage often comes from the tongue falling back, a narrow airway, low muscle tone, jaw positioning, or soft palate collapse.

Oral appliances target these root causes by:

- Moving the jaw forward to widen the airway
- Preventing the tongue from collapsing backward
- Improving airflow circulation
- Stabilizing the jaw and soft tissues
- Supporting healthy breathing patterns

By addressing the structural contributors, oral appliances improve breathing and sleep quality without forced air pressure or bulky equipment.

## Comparing Oral Appliance Therapy vs. CPAP in Austin

Both CPAP and oral appliances treat sleep apnea, but they work differently.

Oral appliance advantages:

- Quiet and unobtrusive
- Comfortable and easy to wear
- No mask, hoses, or pressure settings
- Portable and ideal for travel
- Easier long-term compliance
- More natural breathing experience
- Preferred for mild to moderate OSA
- Effective for snoring and mouth breathing issues

Challenges of CPAP that lead Austin patients to seek alternatives:

- Mask discomfort
- Feeling claustrophobic
- Dry mouth or nasal irritation
- Noise from the machine
- Difficulty maintaining a seal
- Trouble traveling with bulky equipment

Many patients simply cannot tolerate CPAP, making oral appliance therapy a highly effective alternative.
