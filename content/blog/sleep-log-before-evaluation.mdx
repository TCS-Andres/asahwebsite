## What Is a Sleep Diary, and Why Do Sleep Physicians Ask for One?

A sleep diary, also called a sleep log, is a simple nightly record of when you went to bed, how you slept, and what happened around it during the day. The Sleep Foundation describes it as a daily record that gives a physician more specific, reliable information than a general impression of sleeping poorly.

Clinical guidance from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine treats a sleep diary as a standard part of a baseline evaluation, especially before insomnia treatment begins, and notes that patients are commonly asked to keep one for one to two weeks. A diary will not diagnose sleep apnea or any other sleep disorder. Only a physician can do that, usually through a sleep study. If your physician has also mentioned a home sleep test or an in-lab study, our guide to [home sleep tests versus in-lab sleep studies](/home-sleep-test-vs-lab-study/) walks through what each one measures. What a diary adds is a dated, specific picture of your week instead of a memory that blurs after a few nights.

## What to Track Each Night: A Simple 7-Night Sleep Log

You do not need a special app or a complicated chart. A notebook by the bed, or a note on your phone, works fine. For seven nights, jot down:

- The time you got into bed and the time you actually turned out the light
- Roughly how long it took you to fall asleep
- Any times you woke during the night, and about how long you were awake
- The time you woke for good and the time you got out of bed
- Any naps during the day, and about how long they lasted
- Caffeine, alcohol, or any medications, with a rough time
- How rested you felt in the morning and how your energy held up in the afternoon
- Anything a partner mentioned overnight, such as snoring, gasping, restlessness, or pauses in breathing

Keep the entries short. A phrase is enough, such as noting you were up twice but fell back asleep fast, or that a partner said the snoring stopped and started. The goal is a pattern across a week, not a perfect record of any single night.

## What Your Sleep Diary Can Reveal About Your Airway

### Patterns you might not notice on your own

A week on paper often shows things that are easy to miss night to night. Weekday bedtimes may drift later than you realized. Naps may run longest on the days you feel worst. A partner's notes about snoring may cluster on nights you had wine with dinner. None of that is a diagnosis, but it gives you and your physician real material to work with instead of guesswork.

## When It's Worth an Evaluation

Loud, regular snoring is worth a look, especially if your diary or a partner's notes show pauses, gasping, or restlessness alongside it. Waking unrefreshed after what looks like a full night on paper, or feeling foggy through the afternoon on most days of your log, is also worth mentioning to your doctor. If you are not sure whether what you are noticing is worth a conversation, our [adult sleep and airway screening](/sleep-apnea-test/adult/) is a calm, educational starting point.

You do not need a finished two week diary to start. Even a few nights of notes give Dr. Culotta something concrete to build from, and it is never too late to bring your questions in. Awareness is the first step, and starting the log itself is often that first step.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should I keep a sleep diary before my sleep evaluation?

Most physicians ask for one to two weeks of entries, though even a few nights of notes are useful if your appointment is close. Clinical guidance from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine describes one to two weeks as the typical window before treatment decisions are made.

### Does my doctor actually use the sleep diary I fill out?

Yes, a sleep diary gives your physician dated, specific details instead of a general impression, and many patients also use it to spot their own patterns. Notes about snoring or restlessness lining up with certain nights, for example, can be useful to bring up out loud.

### What exactly should I write down each night?

Track your bedtime, how long it took to fall asleep, any awakenings, your final wake time, naps, caffeine or alcohol, and how rested you felt. A short phrase for each entry is enough, and consistency across a week matters more than detail on any single night.

### Can a bed partner's notes count as part of my sleep diary?

Yes, a partner's observations about snoring, gasping, or pauses in breathing are a useful addition to your own notes, since you cannot observe your own breathing while asleep. Add a line for whatever they mention, even briefly.

### Is a sleep diary the same as a sleep study?

No, a sleep diary is a subjective record you keep yourself at home, while a sleep study is a physician ordered overnight test that measures your breathing and oxygen levels. A diary can support a sleep study by giving context, but only a physician diagnoses sleep apnea, through a sleep study.
