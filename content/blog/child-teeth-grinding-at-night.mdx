## The Sound Coming From Your Child's Room

It is a school night in late October. The backpack is packed, the lights are out, and as you pass your child's door you hear it: a gritty, squeaky scrape, like someone chewing gravel. Your child is asleep and grinding their teeth.

If you have been wondering about your child grinding teeth at night, you are in good company. It is one of the most common things parents notice after bedtime, and it often shows up when life gets full, like mid-semester, with tests, projects, practices, and shifting friendships.

Most of the time, grinding is not a reason for alarm. It is information. Here, Austin Sleep & Airway Health walks through why kids grind, how sleep and breathing fit in, what to track at home, and when a closer look makes sense.

## Is It Normal for a Child to Grind Their Teeth at Night?

Often, yes. Teeth grinding (the clinical term is bruxism) is common in childhood, and Nemours KidsHealth notes that most kids outgrow it as they lose their baby teeth.

Estimates of how common it is vary widely: a 2014 systematic review in the Dental Press Journal of Orthodontics found reported rates in children ranging from about 6 percent to nearly 50 percent, largely depending on how each study defined and measured grinding.

A 2018 international consensus paper on bruxism notes that in otherwise healthy people, grinding is better understood as a behavior than a disorder, which makes the more useful question what might be driving it for your child, and whether it is causing trouble for their teeth, jaw, or sleep.

## Why Do Kids Grind Their Teeth in Their Sleep?

There is rarely one single cause: a 2021 literature review described childhood grinding as complex, with likely reasons falling into three broad groups.

### A mouth that is still changing

Between about ages 6 and 12, baby teeth loosen while permanent teeth arrive, keeping the bite in flux. Children's Hospital of Philadelphia links grinding most often to teething or misaligned teeth, and KidsHealth adds pain, such as an earache, as another common trigger.

### Stress and a full semester

KidsHealth points to everyday stress such as a test, a change in routine, or arguments at home, and a 2023 systematic review in Frontiers in Oral Health linked higher anxiety in children and their mothers, plus learning and behavior challenges, to grinding, flagging heavy screen time as a predictor. That does not mean a grinding child is anxious or unhappy: plenty of relaxed, thriving kids grind too, and a busy season is simply one piece of the picture.

### Sleep and breathing

This is where our work focuses: the same Frontiers review linked parent-reported grinding to sleep-disordered breathing, enlarged tonsils, restricted tongue mobility, and nasal obstruction.

## Can Teeth Grinding Be a Sign of a Breathing Problem?

It can be one clue, but it is not proof of anything on its own. A systematic review in the journal Oral Diseases, which analyzed 29 studies, concluded that grinding and sleep-related breathing problems are associated in children and teens, though the evidence is low and no study showed which one comes first.

Some researchers propose that jaw activity during sleep may, in certain children, be part of the body's response to a narrowed airway, one reason bruxism experts now describe grinding as a possible risk factor for some and a possibly protective one for others.

Grinding by itself tells you very little. Alongside other sleep signs, such as snoring on most nights, mouth breathing, pauses or gasps, restless sleep, or a child who wakes tired or cranky, it tells you more. If you have already been [listening for snoring at night](/child-snoring-what-it-means/), grinding belongs in the same notes.

## What You Can Do Now: Keep a Two-Week Grinding Log

You do not need answers yet to be helpful. A simple log turns a worried hunch into something your child's dentist or doctor can use. For about two weeks, jot down:

* **Nights and timing.** Which nights you hear grinding, and roughly when.
* **What comes with it.** Snoring, mouth breathing, pauses, gasps, or lots of tossing and turning.
* **Mornings.** Complaints of a sore jaw, face, or head, or real trouble waking up.
* **The day before.** A big test, a tough practice, a late night, or extra screen time before bed.
* **A short recording.** A minute of audio or video from the doorway, sound on.

A calm wind-down helps too. KidsHealth suggests a warm bath, soothing music, or a book before bed, plus a gentle check-in about what might be on your child's mind. If you hear grinding, there is no need to wake them: just note the time and let them sleep.

## When Child Teeth Grinding Is Worth an Evaluation

Occasional grinding during a hectic week often fades as life calms down. It is worth scheduling a look when grinding happens on most nights for several weeks, when a child older than 6 regularly wakes with a sore jaw, face, or headache, or when a dentist notices tooth sensitivity or worn enamel.

Grinding that comes with snoring, mouth breathing, or gasps deserves attention sooner. The Raising Children Network, a parenting resource in Australia, advises seeing a doctor when a child grinds during sleep and also snores loudly, breathes through an open mouth, or chokes or gasps. Only a physician can diagnose a sleep-related breathing problem, usually through an overnight sleep study, and your pediatrician can involve an ENT or sleep physician.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will my child grow out of teeth grinding?

Many children outgrow teeth grinding as their baby teeth give way to permanent ones, but not every child does. If grinding continues past age 6 with jaw soreness, headaches, or worn teeth, a dental visit makes sense.

### Should I wake my child up when they grind their teeth?

There is usually no need to wake your child, since grinding happens during sleep and waking them mainly interrupts rest. Instead, note the time and whether snoring or pauses came with it.

### Does teeth grinding mean my child has worms?

Teeth grinding alone is not a reliable sign of worms, despite the old saying, since it has far more common explanations. A 2008 study in the Journal of Dentistry for Children found no link between grinding and intestinal parasites in the children evaluated.

### Is my child grinding their teeth because they are anxious?

Not necessarily, since plenty of calm, happy kids grind their teeth, but stress is one recognized contributor worth gently exploring. A relaxed bedtime and an easy chat about school or friends are good places to start.

### Does my child need a night guard for teeth grinding?

Some children benefit from a custom night guard when grinding wears down teeth or causes soreness, but many never need one. KidsHealth notes a dentist may recommend a guard molded to the child's teeth in those cases, though it is worth also asking why the grinding is happening.
