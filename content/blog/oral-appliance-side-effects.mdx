## The Jaw Ache Nobody Mentioned at Your Fitting

You wake up, reach for the appliance case on the nightstand, and notice your jaw feels tight before your eyes are fully open. By the time you are brushing your teeth, you are wondering whether this is just how oral appliance therapy feels, or whether something is off.

Oral appliance side effects are common in the first weeks of treatment, and most of them are a normal part of your jaw and teeth adjusting to a new position every night. A smaller set of symptoms are less common and worth a call to your dentist rather than a wait and see approach.

This guide from Austin Sleep & Airway Health walks through what counts as a typical adjustment, what is worth a phone call, what to track in the early weeks, and how Dr. Culotta and her team keep an eye on your fit over time.

## Oral Appliance Side Effects That Are Normal at First

Most of what people notice in the first two to four weeks is annoying, not alarming. Your jaw, teeth, and salivary glands are all reacting to something new happening every night, and for most people, that reaction eases as the mouth adapts.

### Morning jaw soreness and stiffness

A dull ache or stiffness in your jaw when you first wake up is one of the most common oral appliance side effects, since your muscles are holding a slightly forward position for hours at a stretch. Gentle jaw stretches, a warm washcloth on the area, and a little time after you take the appliance out often help it fade before breakfast.

### Extra saliva or a dry mouth

Some people notice more saliva than usual, even a damp spot on the pillow, while others wake up with a dry mouth instead. Either reaction is a known response to a new appliance, and for most people it settles within the first few weeks.

### Tender teeth or a bite that feels different in the morning

Your back teeth may feel tender, or your bite may feel slightly off for a few minutes after you remove the appliance, since your jaw has been held forward all night and needs a moment to settle back. This brief, morning only change is common and usually resolves once you are up and moving.

## Oral Appliance Side Effects That Are Not Normal

A smaller group of symptoms fall outside a typical adjustment, and they are worth mentioning to your dentist rather than waiting out on your own.

### Sharp, one-sided, or worsening jaw pain

Pain that is sharp rather than achy, on one side, or getting worse over a few weeks is different from ordinary morning stiffness. New clicking, popping, or a jaw that catches when you open wide is also worth describing at your next visit, or sooner if uncomfortable.

### A bite that will not settle back

If your teeth do not feel like they meet the way they used to, even later in the day well after the appliance is out, that is different from the brief morning adjustment above. Research on long term oral appliance use has found that lasting bite changes are one reason some patients consider stopping treatment, which is why regular dental check ins matter.

### Appliance damage or a fit that has changed

A cracked appliance, a loose piece, or a fit that suddenly feels tighter or looser than a month ago is not something to adjust around on your own. Appliances are built to be adjusted by the dentist who fitted them, not reshaped or forced.

## Why These Oral Appliance Side Effects Happen

An oral appliance works by holding your lower jaw slightly forward, which can help keep the airway open while you sleep. That forward position is new for your jaw joints, chewing muscles, and teeth, so some reaction in the early weeks is expected rather than a sign something has gone wrong.

Dentists who provide oral appliance therapy typically start conservatively and adjust gradually, a process called titration, rather than moving the jaw to its final position on night one. Joint guidance from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the American Academy of Dental Sleep Medicine calls for a qualified dentist to oversee this process and watch for dental side effects or bite changes, which is part of why follow-up visits are built into [how oral appliance therapy works](/how-oral-appliance-therapy-works-austins-most-comfortable-alternative-to-cpap/).

## What to Track During Your First Weeks

A little tracking gives your dentist something specific to respond to instead of a general sense that something feels off. Over your first two to four weeks, jot down when jaw soreness happens and how long it lasts, whether your bite feels different and for how long, any drooling or dry mouth and whether it is easing week to week, and anything new, like a click or a spot that rubs.

Bring this log to your fitting and follow-up visits. Our [patient resources](/patient-resources/) page has more on preparing for an appointment, and specific details make it easier for your dentist to tell whether an adjustment is needed.

## When It Is Worth an Evaluation

Most oral appliance side effects settle within the first month, and mild morning soreness alone is usually not a reason to stop wearing your appliance. It is worth scheduling a visit if soreness has not eased after several weeks, if pain is sharp or one sided, if your bite feels different well into the day, or if the appliance feels loose, cracked, or different from when it was fitted.

It is also worth a check in if you simply are not sure your appliance is doing its job. A follow-up [sleep test](/home-sleep-test-vs-lab-study/) coordinated with your physician is the only way to confirm how well an oral appliance is controlling sleep apnea, separate from how it feels in your mouth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does jaw soreness from an oral appliance last?

Morning jaw soreness typically eases within the first two to four weeks as your muscles and joints adjust to the new position. Gentle stretching and a warm compress after you remove the appliance often help it fade faster.

### Is it normal to drool or have a dry mouth with an oral appliance?

Yes, a change in saliva, either more or less than usual, is a common reaction to a new oral appliance and usually settles within the first few weeks. If dryness or drooling has not improved after about a month, mention it at your next follow-up visit.

### Will an oral appliance move my teeth or change my bite permanently?

Most people notice only brief, morning only bite changes that resolve on their own, but long term use can occasionally lead to more lasting shifts in some patients. Regular follow-ups catch these changes early.

### How do I know if my jaw pain is normal or something to worry about?

Dull, morning only soreness that fades within an hour or so is typical, while sharp, one sided, or worsening pain is not. New clicking, popping, or trouble opening your mouth comfortably is worth describing to your dentist rather than waiting out.

### How long does it take to get used to wearing an oral appliance?

Many people need a few weeks to feel comfortable, since dentists typically introduce the appliance gradually and adjust it in stages instead of fitting it to its final position right away. Wearing it during quiet time before bed the first few nights can help you adjust.

### Can I stop wearing my oral appliance if my jaw hurts?

Talk with your dentist before stopping, since mild soreness is usually manageable and often resolves with a small adjustment rather than requiring you to give up treatment. If pain is significant or does not ease, your dentist can adjust the fit or the level of advancement instead.
