## A Click You Almost Ignore

It is a warm spring evening in Austin, patio string lights on, plates going around the table. You open wide for a big bite and catch it: a small pop right in front of your ear. Nobody else notices. You almost do not either, until it happens again a few bites later.

Jaw clicking like this is one of the most common things people notice about their own body and one of the least talked about. It can show up for years without pain, quietly, then suddenly feel worth a second thought.

At Austin Sleep & Airway Health, we hear about jaw clicking often, usually from people who are curious rather than alarmed. This post covers what causes clicking and popping in the jaw, when it is common, and when it is worth a closer look.

## What Jaw Clicking and Popping Actually Means

Your temporomandibular joints sit just in front of each ear, where your lower jaw hinges and slides against your skull. A small disc of cartilage sits inside each joint and normally glides smoothly with the jaw as you open, close, and slide side to side.

In many people, that disc shifts slightly out of its usual resting position. When you open your mouth, the disc catches, then slips back into place, and that small movement is what you hear as a click or pop. The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research (NIDCR) notes that clicking or popping sounds in the jaw joints, without pain, are common, are considered normal, and usually do not need treatment.

That single fact surprises a lot of people. A sound alone, especially one you have had for a while without pain, is not automatically a problem to fix.

## Why Does Your Jaw Click When You Chew or Yawn?

Clicking often shows up during wide movements: a big bite, a long yawn, or a lengthy dental appointment, because those movements ask the joint and disc to travel further than usual. MedlinePlus lists clicking, popping, or a grating sound as one of the more common signs of a temporomandibular disorder (TMD), alongside jaw pain, trouble chewing, headaches, and a jaw that catches or locks, though it notes the exact cause is often unclear.

Several everyday habits get blamed for jaw clicking, and some of that reputation is deserved. Jaw clenching, especially during stress, and grinding at night can add extra pressure on the joint. Frequent gum chewing works the joint harder too, though plenty of people click without any of these habits.

Braces and bite alignment often get blamed for jaw clicking too, but NIDCR notes that the idea that a bad bite or orthodontic treatment causes TMD is not supported by current evidence. If clicking appears during orthodontic treatment, it is worth mentioning to your orthodontist.

## Is Jaw Clicking Something to Worry About?

For most people, the answer is no, not on its own. NIDCR and the American Dental Association both describe a conservative approach to TMJ concerns, often summarized as less is often best, starting with simple self care before anything more involved. A painless click that has been there for years, without other symptoms, generally falls into that low concern category.

What changes the picture is a jaw that stops moving the way it used to. Locking, meaning your jaw briefly will not open or close all the way, is a different signal than a simple click. MedlinePlus also flags trouble chewing, jaw pain, ear pain, and headaches as symptoms worth mentioning to a provider, especially when a few show up together.

A few patterns deserve closer attention: clicking after a jaw injury, clicking on one side with a bite that feels different, or a click that used to be quiet and now hurts. None of these automatically mean something is wrong. They mean it is worth a look, not a guess.

## What You Can Do Now If Your Jaw Clicks

Before any appointment, a little noticing helps. Over a week or two, jot down what you observe:

- When the click happens: chewing, yawning, talking, or first thing in the morning
- Whether it hurts, and if so, where
- Any catching, or a moment where your jaw feels briefly stuck
- Habits you notice, like gum chewing, nail biting, or clenching during focused work
- Whether a partner has mentioned grinding sounds at night, or you wake up with a sore jaw

A few gentle habits can also ease strain on the joint while you decide whether an evaluation makes sense: softer foods for a stretch, smaller bites, less gum, and noticing when you clench during the day. This is not about fixing the click. It is about giving the joint less to react to while you watch the pattern.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is jaw clicking without pain something to worry about?

Painless jaw clicking is common and usually does not need treatment, according to NIDCR, especially when it has been present for a while without other symptoms. Keep an eye on whether pain, locking, or a change in your bite ever joins the sound, since that combination is what shifts things toward an evaluation.

### Why does my jaw click when I chew or yawn?

Jaw clicking during chewing or yawning usually happens because a small disc inside the joint shifts slightly and then slips back into place during wide movements. Everyday habits like gum chewing, clenching, and nail biting can make the sound more noticeable, though they are not the only reason it happens.

### Can stress or clenching cause jaw clicking?

Stress-related clenching can add extra pressure on the jaw joint and may make an existing click more noticeable, though it does not automatically cause clicking on its own. Noticing when you clench, such as during driving or focused work, is a useful first step before any appointment.

### Does jaw clicking go away on its own?

Some jaw clicking fades over time, some stays mild for years without changing, and some sticks around indefinitely without ever becoming painful. Tracking what you notice over a few weeks is more useful than guessing at an outcome.

### Is jaw clicking a reason to see an orthodontist or get a night guard?

Jaw clicking alone is not usually a reason to start orthodontic treatment or a night guard, since NIDCR notes that braces and bite alignment are not established causes of jaw joint problems. A night guard may still help if clenching is part of the picture, which an evaluation can sort out.

### When does jaw clicking mean something more serious, like locking?

Jaw clicking becomes more serious when your jaw briefly will not open or close all the way, which is called locking and differs from a simple sound. New clicking after an injury, or clicking that now comes with pain, is also worth mentioning at a visit.
