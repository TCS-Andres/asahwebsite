## The Extra Hour That Didn't Feel Like One

Early Sunday, Nov. 2, the clocks fell back, and you were promised an extra hour. Now it's Tuesday. You woke before your alarm anyway, it was dark on the drive home, and by midafternoon your focus is gone.

If your daylight saving time sleep feels off, you're in good company. A one-hour shift sounds small, but your body keeps its own schedule, and it doesn't reset just because your phone did.

At Austin Sleep & Airway Health, we look at sleep through breathing and the airway, so the time change makes a useful checkpoint. This post covers why falling back leaves you tired, how long adjusting takes, and the signs worth a closer look.

## Why Am I So Tired After Gaining an Hour?

The short answer: your wall clock moved, but your body clock did not. Your internal rhythm takes its cues mostly from light, and for a few days it keeps running on the old schedule.

### Your body wakes with the sun

After the change, sunrise arrives an hour earlier by the clock. Mass General Brigham sleep specialists note this can wake people before they meant to get up, erasing the extra hour. Opening your eyes at 5 a.m. instead of 6 is a common pattern.

### Early risers often feel it most

A UCSF expert on body clocks explains that falling back is easier on night owls and harder on morning larks, already primed to wake early. Going into the weekend short on sleep can make it worse.

### Darker evenings change your routine

Evenings now get dark about an hour sooner. That often means less time outside, dozing on the couch after dinner, and a bedtime that drifts, just a routine in transition.

## How Long Does It Take to Adjust to the Fall Time Change?

For most adults, not long. The CDC's National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health notes it can take about one week for the body to adjust to new times for sleeping, eating, and activity. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) adds that standard time lines up best with human circadian biology, since it helps sync the body clock with sunrise and sunset. The switch, not the destination, is the problem.

Texas lawmakers passed a bill in 2025 to keep the state on daylight saving time year-round if Congress allows it, so Austin's clocks still change twice a year for now. That gives you a checkpoint: still dragging two weeks out is a sign the clock isn't the whole story.

## Daylight Saving Time Sleep Tips for This Week

Small, steady cues help your body catch up, no special products needed.

### Get outside in the morning

Step outside soon after waking, even for a few minutes with your coffee. Morning daylight is one of the strongest signals your body clock uses to set the day's timing.

### Keep your wake time steady

Pick a wake time and hold it, weekends included. Sleeping in on Saturday can pull your rhythm back toward the old schedule. If you're waking too early, blackout curtains or a sleep mask can help.

### Move dinner, caffeine, and screens earlier

Experts at Mass General Brigham suggest an earlier dinner and keeping caffeine to the morning or early afternoon. Dimming lights and setting screens aside before bed gives your body a clearer signal the day is ending.

### Keep naps short and early

The late-afternoon slump is real this week. Keep any rest brief and well before evening so it doesn't push bedtime later.

## Your Two-Week Sleep Check: What to Track

While your body adjusts, a few notes each morning can show whether things are settling. Jot down:

* Bedtime and wake time, and roughly how long you slept.
* How rested you felt on waking, on a simple 1 to 5 scale.
* Whether you snored, or had pauses, gasps, or choking sounds a partner noticed.
* Morning dry mouth, headaches, or a sore, tired jaw.
* How often you woke up, including trips to the bathroom.
* Afternoon energy and focus.

NHLBI notes a sleep diary like this can help your provider understand how long and well you sleep and how sleepy you feel during the day. If you wear a smartwatch, its data can add to the picture, and our guide on [what to do after a smartwatch sleep apnea alert](/smartwatch-sleep-apnea-alert/) explains how to use it without over-reading it.

## Clock Tired or Something Else? Signs That Outlast the Time Change

By the second week, most time-change tiredness has faded. If yours hasn't, ask what kind of tired it is.

### You're getting enough hours but still wake unrefreshed

The CDC recommends seven or more hours nightly for most adults and lists feeling tired despite enough sleep as one sign your sleep deserves attention. If Sunday's bonus hour and a steady week made no dent, that's worth noticing.

### Your nights include snoring or pauses

According to NHLBI, signs of sleep apnea include loud snoring, breathing that stops and starts, gasping, dry mouth, headaches, and waking often to urinate. Many people don't know until someone tells them. Our guide to [how snoring differs from sleep apnea](/snoring-vs-sleep-apnea/) can help you make sense of what a partner hears.

### Shorter days are affecting your mood

NIMH notes a winter pattern of seasonal mood changes often begins in late fall, with signs such as oversleeping, low energy, and pulling away from activities. If darker evenings weigh on you more than usual, raise it with a provider too.

## When Lingering Tiredness Is Worth an Evaluation

Two weeks after the change, it's worth a closer look if you're sleeping seven or more hours and still waking unrefreshed, if someone hears you snore or notices pauses or gasping, or if dry mouth, headaches, or bathroom trips keep showing up in your notes, along with daytime drowsiness that disrupts work or driving.

These signs are not a diagnosis. Only a physician can diagnose sleep apnea, through a home sleep test or an in-lab sleep study, and our comparison of [home sleep tests and in-lab studies](/home-sleep-test-vs-lab-study/) explains how each works. Bring your two-week notes; they turn vague tiredness into something specific.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why am I more tired after gaining an hour of sleep?

The extra hour often disappears because your body clock still runs on the old schedule, so you may wake early and sleep less overall. Morning daylight and a steady wake time help your rhythm catch up.

### How long does it take to adjust to the time change?

Most adults adjust within about a week, matching CDC NIOSH guidance on clock changes. Early risers and people already short on sleep may need a little longer.

### Why do I keep waking up an hour early since the clocks changed?

Sunrise now comes an hour earlier by the clock, and a body used to waking with the light may keep doing so for days. Blackout curtains and a consistent bedtime can help you settle in.

### Can the time change make sleep apnea worse?

The one-hour clock change isn't known to cause sleep apnea, but a week of choppier sleep can make existing tiredness harder to ignore. If you already use CPAP or an oral appliance, keep using it through the transition and mention lingering changes to your physician.

### Is tiredness after the clocks change a sign of seasonal depression?

Not usually, since most time-change tiredness fades within about a week, but low mood and energy that linger as days shorten are worth raising with a provider. NIMH notes winter-pattern seasonal changes often begin in late fall.
